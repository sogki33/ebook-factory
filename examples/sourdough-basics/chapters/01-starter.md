# Your First Starter

A sourdough starter is just flour and water that wild yeast and lactic-acid bacteria have moved into. You don't buy it, you **grow** it — and in about seven days of two-minute feedings it will be strong enough to raise a loaf.

## What you need

You need no special equipment. A clean glass jar, a spoon and a kitchen scale are enough. Measuring by weight instead of cups is the single habit that makes sourdough predictable.

| Item | Why it matters | Budget option |
|---|---|---|
| Glass jar (~1 L) | You can see bubbles and the rise line | Any washed jam jar |
| Kitchen scale | Feedings are ratios by weight | A $10 digital scale |
| Whole-wheat or rye flour | More wild yeast, faster start | Mix 50/50 with white flour |
| Lukewarm water | Chlorine slows microbes | Leave tap water out overnight |
| Rubber band | Marks the level after feeding | A strip of tape |

> [!TIP] Start on a weekend
> Day 1 takes ten minutes. After that, each feeding takes two. Starting on a Saturday means the first loaf lands the following weekend.

## The seven-day feeding plan

Mix **50 g flour + 50 g water** in the jar, cover loosely and leave it at room temperature (ideally 24–26 °C). From day 2 onward, discard all but 50 g and feed again with 50 g flour and 50 g water.

![Feed once a day: keep 50 g, add 50 g flour and 50 g water, stir and mark the level](images/starter-feeding.jpg)

| Day | What you'll see | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Nothing yet | Mix and cover |
| 2 | A few bubbles, maybe a sharp smell | Discard to 50 g, feed |
| 3–4 | Sudden rise, then a quiet day | Keep feeding — the quiet day is normal |
| 5–6 | Regular rise, yogurt-like smell | Feed twice a day if it's warm |
| 7 | Doubles within 4–8 h of feeding | It's ready to bake with |

> [!WARNING] Day 3 false alarm
> A burst of activity on day 2 or 3 is often from bacteria that die off. When the jar goes quiet, don't throw it away — keep feeding on schedule.

## Is it ready? The float test and better signs

The famous float test (drop a spoonful into water) is unreliable for whole-grain starters. Use these signs together instead.

- [ ] It at least **doubles** within 4–8 hours of a feeding
- [ ] The top is domed and full of bubbles, and the sides show a web of holes
- [ ] It smells pleasantly sour — like yogurt or apples, not like nail polish
- [ ] It does this **two days in a row**

![A ready starter: domed top, bubbles all the way down the glass](images/starter-ready.jpg)

## Troubleshooting

> [!DONT] Don't seal the jar
> Gas needs a way out. Rest the lid on top or use a cloth; a sealed jar can crack.

> [!NOTE]
> A grey liquid on top ("hooch") just means the starter is hungry. Pour it off or stir it in, then feed.

Pink or orange streaks, or fuzzy mould, are different: throw the whole thing away, wash the jar with hot water and start again. Everything else — slow starts, odd smells in week one, a flat day — is normal and fixable with warmth and regular feedings.
